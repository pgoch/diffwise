# How it works

This page describes version 0.3. Diffwise compares two HTML documents in five steps.

## 1. Clean and parse

Each version is cleaned with [DOMPurify](https://github.com/cure53/DOMPurify) and then parsed by the browser's `DOMParser`, which builds an inert document: no script runs and no image loads. This makes HTML from users or other systems safe to compare.

- **Removed:** scripts, event handlers such as `onclick`, `javascript:` links, `<style>` elements, and attributes that take focus or open popovers. Forms and dialogs are removed, but their content stays. Comments and hidden elements are left out, and so are attributes that start with `data-diffwise-`, so the content cannot pose as details of a change.
- **Kept**, so the content looks as it did in its editor: classes, inline styles that change only the look (such as color, font, alignment, spacing and borders, but no positioning and no `url()`), and checkboxes, which become read-only.
- **Links** that open a new tab get `rel="noopener noreferrer"`.
- **Ids** get a prefix of their own in each diff and each version, and in-page links follow them. Footnotes work within each version, and the ids never clash with the ones of your app.

## 2. Read the text and its format

Diffwise compares the text a reader sees, not the markup. A run of whitespace, including non-breaking spaces, counts as one space, except in `<pre>` and `<textarea>`. Blocks, images and line breaks separate words, and an image is one character of the text, so it is compared like a word. With `ignoreWhitespace`, all whitespace is ignored, and with `ignoreCase`, letter case.

Each piece of text also keeps its format: its formatting and the address of the link around it. Formatting is what the HTML says, not how a tag looks by default, since Diffwise cannot know how your app styles a heading or a class. So `<h2><b>Pricing</b></h2>` and `<h2>Pricing</h2>` differ in bold.

- **Formatting** comes from tags (bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, superscript, subscript, code, `<mark>` and `<small>`), from the heading level, and from the `style` attribute: text and background color, font, font size, weight, italic, underline and strikethrough, letter case, and the alignment of a block. The old `<font color face size>`, `align` and `<center>`, still found in emails, count too. Spacing, borders and dimensions do not, and neither do `class`, `title`, `datetime` or `id`.
- **The same look written another way is no change:** `<b>` and `<strong>`, `<i>` and `<em>`, or a color written as `#c0392b` and as `rgb(192, 57, 43)`. As in CSS, the nearest element decides the weight, the italic and the letter case, so `<b style="font-weight: normal">`, which Google Docs puts around pasted text, is not bold. Underline and strikethrough add up. A transparent background is no background.
- **Links** are compared by their address (`href`) as written. `target`, `rel` and `title` do not count. In-page links, such as footnotes, are compared without the prefix of their version.
- **Images** are compared by their file (`src`), their size and their alternative text. The size comes from `style`, or else from the `width` and `height` attributes, as in CSS, so `width="480"` and `style="width: 480px"` are the same. In the alternative text, single spaces count, as a screen reader reads it, and `ignoreCase` and `ignoreWhitespace` apply; a missing `alt` and an empty one differ. `srcset`, `class` and `title` do not count. Changes of other elements without text inside a line, such as `<svg>`, `<video>` or form fields, are not detected; the checkbox of a list item is the exception.
- **List markers** are read as the browser shows them. The style comes from `list-style-type` in `style`, or from `type`, of the item, the elements around it and the list, inherited as in CSS. The style, not the tag, says whether a list is bulleted or numbered: a `<ul>` with `list-style-type: decimal` shows numbers, so it is a numbered list. A list with the style `none` shows no markers, so its items, like paragraphs, have no marker to compare. Numbers are counted as the HTML standard counts them, with `start`, `value` and `reversed`, and the level says how deep an item is nested.
- **Checkboxes** count at the start of a list item, also inside a `<label>`, as in the task lists of GitHub, Notion or Markdown editors. A checkbox elsewhere, such as in a paragraph or a table cell, is not compared.

## 3. Split into lines

HTML has no real lines, so Diffwise uses blocks instead. A paragraph or a heading is one line, also inside a quote or a `<div>`, which are split into the blocks they hold.

- **Lists.** Each item of a list is a line, also in a nested list. In a definition list, each term and each description is a line. An item that holds blocks, such as a nested list or several paragraphs, is split further, and its marker stands at its first line. Each line holds copies of the lists around its item, and the copy of the item gets its number in `value`, so the item keeps its indentation and its number. An empty item is a line too, as its marker shows. `<li><p>Text</p></li>`, as Google Docs and many editors write it, is the same item as `<li>Text</li>`.
- **Tables.** The caption and each row of a table are lines, in the order a browser shows them: the caption, the head, the body and the foot, even when the foot comes first in the HTML. A table in a `<figure>`, as CKEditor writes it, is split too, and the caption of the figure is a line. A row of empty cells is a line, as its cells show, but a row without cells is not. A list or a table inside a cell stays part of its row.
- **One line each:** a code block, a `<details>` element and a figure with an image.

A line a reader would not see, such as an empty paragraph, is left out. The `id` of a list or a table stays in its first line only, so a link to it leads to its start. The lines of each version are numbered from 1.

## 4. Compare

- **Lines.** The lines of both versions are matched with the Myers algorithm, the one git uses by default. Two lines are equal when they have the same structure, text, formatting, links and images. A heading counts as a paragraph here, as its level is formatting, so a heading that becomes a paragraph is a changed line. The formatting of whitespace does not count, such as bold that also covers the space after a word. A list item is compared like a paragraph, without its marker, which is compared once the lines are matched. A table row equals another only with the same cells: the same text in the same columns, so text moved into the empty cell next to it is a change.
- **Changed lines.** Where the versions differ, a removed and an added line are paired as one changed line when they share at least half of their words. An image counts as two words: one that all images share and one for its file, so a paragraph whose image was replaced stays one changed line. Of the possible pairings, the one with the highest total similarity wins, and the order of both versions stays. The other lines are removed or added. A moved paragraph shows as a removal and an addition.
- **Words.** In a changed line, the text is compared word by word, again with the Myers algorithm. Words come from the browser's `Intl.Segmenter`, so languages written without spaces, such as Chinese and Japanese, are compared correctly. Each punctuation mark is compared on its own, and images are compared with the words, by their file.
- **Formatting and links.** In a changed line, the words that stayed are compared by their format. Each change gets a description: the styles before and after, a changed value such as a color, a heading level, or the old and the new address of a link. Neighboring words with the same change are one change. New text is a change of the text, so its formatting gets no description of its own.
- **Images.** An image removed and an image added between the same unchanged words are one changed image, described by what changed: its file, its size or its alternative text. An image without a pair is added or removed, like a word.
- **List markers.** In each pair of matched lines, also an unchanged one, the markers are compared: the list type, bulleted or numbered; the style, such as numbers → lowercase letters; the number; and the level. A paragraph that became a list item, or the other way around, is another list type. Another list type or level brings its own style, such as the circles of a nested list, so only the type or the level counts then. A pair that differs only in its markers becomes a changed line.
- **Item numbers.** A number counts only when the place of the item in its list does not explain it. When an item is added or removed, the items after it have other numbers in the two versions, but only the added or removed item is marked, also when an editor wrote the new numbers into `value`. A list that starts at another number, or an item with another number of its own, has its markers marked.
- **Checkboxes.** A checkbox of a list item that was checked or cleared makes the line changed. A checkbox in only one of the two lines does not count.
- **Table columns.** Before the rows are compared, the columns of the old and the new table are matched, once for the whole table. Two columns score a point for each pair of matched rows in which both hold a cell with the same text; empty cells and cells across several columns do not count. Of the matchings that keep the order of the columns, the one with the highest total score wins. Columns left between two matched ones are paired by position when both tables have as many of them, such as a column in which every value changed; otherwise they are removed and added. As the columns are matched for the whole table, an added column is the same column in every row, even where values such as "Unlimited" repeat.
- **Cells.** In a changed row, each cell is compared word by word with the cell of its column in the other version, so a change never runs from one cell into the next: text moved to another cell is removed from one and added to the other. A cell of an added or a removed column is marked whole, in every row, and so is each cell of an added or a removed row. Cells merged into one, or one cell split into several, are compared as a group. Formatting, links and images in cells are compared as in paragraphs.

`ignoreFormatting` leaves out formatting, heading levels and the look of list markers: the list type, the style and the level. Item numbers still count, as readers cite them. `ignoreLinkChanges` leaves out links added, removed or pointing elsewhere. Changes of images and checkboxes always count.

## 5. Mark and display

- Removed words are wrapped in `<del>` and added words in `<ins>`, inside the formatting, so a bold word or a link keeps its look. Text with changed formatting or a changed link gets the same markers, in both versions. Each line is cleaned again after marking.
- A changed image gets a frame instead of a background, and the frame follows the marking style. An added or a removed image is framed only in its version.
- A changed list marker is drawn by Diffwise, as the marker of a browser (`::marker`) takes no background or frame. It is an empty `<del>` or `<ins>` at the start of the item, in place of the browser's marker, with the same text, such as "6." or a bullet, in the font of the item. It is marked like changed words, in each version that has a marker. Diffwise draws numbers, also with a leading zero, Latin and Greek letters, Roman numerals, and dot, circle and square bullets. A marker in a rarer style, such as Armenian numbering, stays the browser's and is not marked; the icon and the tooltip tell the change. The text of a drawn marker comes from CSS, so copied text leaves it out, as it leaves out the browser's marker.
- A checked or cleared checkbox gets a frame, like a changed image.
- Each cell of an added or a removed row or column is marked whole, like changed words; in a changed cell, only the changed words are marked. A table row gets no background of a changed line, so the unchanged cells keep their colors, and the number with its bar shows that the row changed.
- A row with changes besides the text gets an icon in the column between the versions: formatting, link, image, list marker, checkbox, or several changes with their number. Changed text and changed formatting in one line are two markers, and the icon is about the formatting only.
- A tooltip on the icon and on the marked text says what changed, in the language of the interface: "italic → bold", "text color: ■ #c0392b → ■ #1e7d4f", "heading 3 → heading 2", "size: 480 × 270 → 640 × 360 px", "bulleted list → numbered list", "number 5 → number 6", or the old and the new address with the changed part highlighted. A file shows by its name, or by its whole address when both names are the same, and a long `data:` address is shortened. A checkbox has a title only: "Checkbox checked" or "Checkbox cleared".
- There is one tooltip for each diff, in the top layer of the page (the Popover API), so no `overflow` of your app cuts it. It stands under its icon or text, or above it when there is no room below. It shows on hover and on focus from the keyboard; the pointer can move onto it, and Escape or scrolling the page hides it.
- Each row of the view is a CSS grid: the number and the content of the earlier line, the column between the versions, and the number and the content of the later line. Matching lines stay level even when one of them is taller, and both versions scroll together. Opposite an added or a removed line stays an empty place.
- In each version, the rows of a table look like one table. Each row is a copy of the table with that row alone, and hidden copies of all rows of the table, already marked, give it the columns of the whole table. A table of more than 30 rows copies fewer rows: for each column, and for each set of columns that one cell spans, the row with the longest text there and the row with the longest word, so its columns may differ by a few pixels. A row grows to the height of the row opposite, so the table has no holes; a table inside a quote or a list does not grow. A table wider than its column scrolls as one, with the scrollbar under its last row.
- Tables get a look of their own: a frame with rounded corners, lines between the columns, and a tinted background in header rows, which are the rows of `<thead>` and rows of `<th>` cells alone. Styles that the document gives its cells win over it. The caption stands above the table, as wide as the table. A cell across several rows (`rowspan`) shows its content in its first row; in the rows below, its place stays empty, with no line above it. A screen reader hears each row as a table of its own; the accessibility review planned for 0.5 will come back to this.
- The content of a line stays inside its cell, even an element that a class of your app gives a fixed position.
- Colors and sizes come from CSS variables, and the options add modifier classes to the component, so you can adjust the look in your CSS; see [Styling with CSS](configuration.md#styling-with-css).

## Architecture

The diff engine is plain TypeScript with no dependency on Angular, and DOMPurify is its only dependency. It uses the DOM of the browser, so Diffwise does not support server-side rendering yet. The Angular package wraps the engine in the `DiffwiseHtmlDiff` component, `provideDiffwise()` and the `Diffwise` service. The engine describes changes with data, such as the styles before and after, and the component turns the data into the texts of the tooltips, so the engine knows no languages. This keeps the engine easy to test and leaves the door open for other frameworks later.
